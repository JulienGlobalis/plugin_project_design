# Project Canvas Document Structure

Use this default structure when the user supplies no compatible template.
This is a documentary mapping of the validated Canvas, not a second business
contract.

## Opening

Start with:

- the neutral document title `Project Canvas`, optionally followed by the
  project name only when that name is supplied by the Canvas or the user;
- supplied document metadata that helps review, such as artefact baseline,
  review context, language, or confidentiality marking;
- an explicit notice when Provisional or Unresolved content materially
  qualifies the document and that notice already exists in the Canvas.

Omit unsupported metadata rather than displaying invented placeholders.

Do not add a subtitle, tagline, executive summary, open-question count,
traceability summary, approval statement, or other introductory narrative
unless it exists in the Canvas or the user explicitly supplies it as document
metadata. Do not synthesize such text from otherwise accurate Canvas content.

## Required Sections

Represent the ten Canvas sections with a visible heading each:

1. Business Context;
2. Objectives and Expected Value;
3. Project Stakeholders;
4. Users;
5. Functional Scope;
6. Technical Constraints;
7. Risks;
8. Decisions;
9. Questions;
10. Success Criteria.

Keep explicit gaps in their section. Never remove an empty but required
section when the Canvas states that its information is missing, contradictory,
unsupported, or awaiting a Decision.

Within Functional Scope, keep MVP, Outside MVP, and Unresolved Scope visibly
separate. Preserve explicit exclusions and future Options as distinct from
Outside MVP when the artefact distinguishes them.

## Registers

Preserve an existing logical register when it carries status, authority,
qualification, or traceability. A document may change a register's visual
layout but not its rows, meaning, or relationships.

The Canvas Functional Scope (`MVP` and `Outside MVP`), Risks, Decisions, and
Questions tables are mandatory business content: reproduce them with the same
columns, rows, question numbers, and answer cells. Never add a column to them.

Recommended documentary uses include:

- participant tables for stakeholder authority and user interaction;
- Scope tables for position, status, and basis;
- Decision tables for authority, date, consequence, applicability, and basis;
- Risk and Issue tables that preserve their type;
- question tables for impact classification, authority, and next action;
- success tables for criterion, evidence, status, and remaining gap.

Do not force prose into a table when it obscures rationale. Do not split one
logical item across disconnected tables without a clear cross-reference.

## Readiness and Traceability

After the ten sections, preserve any supplied readiness assessment for
functional design, technical design, and backlog preparation. Keep blockers
and qualifications visible.

Preserve the Canvas Knowledge Basis through concise source references,
footnotes, endnotes, or a final traceability section. Do not expose internal
identifiers in a stakeholder-facing document unless they are required for
review or explicitly requested.

When traceability belongs to a separate audit artefact rather than the Canvas,
do not add it to this document unless the user explicitly requests a combined
document.

## Presentation Hierarchy

Use one document title, one heading level for the ten required sections, and
subheadings for stable subsections such as MVP, Outside MVP, and Unresolved
Scope. Keep heading levels sequential.

Use restrained emphasis for statuses and warnings. Do not use color as the
only carrier of status or meaning. Keep repeated labels and register columns
consistent throughout the document.
